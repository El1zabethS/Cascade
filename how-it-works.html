A reverse supply chain for AI compute

Make existing compute useful to someone new.

Cascade connects organizations retiring AI hardware with people who need accessible compute. It starts with workload fit, then coordinates qualification, hosting, and a transparent handoff.

01 // DISCOVER

Describe the work

A team explains what it wants to run, its preferred deployment, expected scale, and budget priorities.

02 // QUALIFY

Verify the asset

A provider lists equipment. A qualified partner checks condition, configuration, ownership, and secure data removal.

03 // MATCH

Connect need to supply

Cascade compares workload requirements with verified equipment and available hosted or on-site options.

04 // OPERATE

Support the next life

The new user gets a clear path to access, support, maintenance, and an end-of-use route for the asset.

A marketplace with coordination

Not just a listing. A better fit.

Ordinary resale is mostly organized around equipment specs and price. Cascade begins with the job to be done: a research group that needs a private model sandbox, a startup that needs inference capacity, or a school that needs hands-on AI hardware.

The product is the match and the confidence around it—not a claim that every older GPU fits every workload. Poor fits should be screened out before a user commits.

Start focused. Prove the loop.

One campus. A few real workloads.

Supply partner

Work with one organization that has known, decommissioned equipment. Confirm provenance, condition, power, and maintenance expectations before listing it.

Demand partner

Interview a small group of labs, student teams, or startups. Learn what they actually run, what they can pay, and whether they need local control or hosted access.

Use a hosting partner

Avoid building a data center for the first pilot. Explore existing campus or regional facilities that can provide power, cooling, network access, and support.

How the business could work

Earn when value is created.

A possible starting model is a transaction or coordination fee when a user books verified compute. Over time, Cascade could add paid qualification, fleet-management, or lifecycle reporting for equipment owners.

These are hypotheses to validate, not established revenue. The first pilot should compare revenue per used GPU-hour with hosting, support, payment, and maintenance costs.
